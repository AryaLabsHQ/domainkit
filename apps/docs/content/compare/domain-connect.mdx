## Verdict

Domain Connect fits when your setup is a fixed template and your customers' DNS providers already
support it. DomainKit fits when the record values differ per customer and you want to write through
the customer's own Cloudflare or Vercel account. Nothing stops you from offering both.

## What Domain Connect is

Domain Connect is an open protocol. A service provider, such as an email or hosting company,
publishes a DNS template. A supporting DNS provider shows the customer a consent screen and applies
the template. The customer never edits records by hand.

The protocol is under active work. Its IETF working-group draft is at revision 03, updated 3 July
2026 ([4](https://datatracker.ietf.org/doc/draft-ietf-dconn-domainconnect/)). The
[Templates repository](https://github.com/Domain-Connect/Templates) was pushed to on the day we
checked, with dozens of open pull requests being reviewed.

## What it asks of you

Getting a template accepted has two steps ([1](https://www.domainconnect.org/getting-started/)).

1. Define the template and get it merged into the shared Templates repository. Submissions need to
   be tested in the online editor and follow strict naming and quality rules.
2. Onboard the template with each DNS provider. Domain Connect's guide says this is done by
   contacting them, and that some may require contractual terms and further review.

The second step repeats for every DNS provider you want to reach, and each one decides on its own
timeline.

## Who supports it

The Domain Connect site lists these providers as live: IONOS, Cloudflare, Domain Chief, Glauca
Digital, GoDaddy, NameSilo, Plesk, Vercel, and WordPress.com
([2](https://www.domainconnect.org/dns-providers/)).

Two details matter for builders. Cloudflare supports the synchronous flow with signed requests, and
asks you to email template links and public-key domains
([5](https://developers.cloudflare.com/dns/reference/domain-connect/)). Vercel supports it only
where Vercel is the domain's authoritative DNS ([6](https://vercel.com/docs/domains/domain-connect)).

Well-known senders use it. Resend's Cloudflare guide offers a Sign in to Cloudflare option that
uses Domain Connect ([7](https://resend.com/docs/dashboard/domains/cloudflare)).

## Side by side

|                                  | Domain Connect                                         | DomainKit                                                             |
| -------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------ | --------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| What it is                       | Open protocol and shared template repository           | Open-source library in your app                                       |
| Who shows the consent screen     | The customer's DNS provider                            | Your app                                                              |
| What you set up per DNS provider | Onboard your template with each provider               | One adapter per provider. Cloudflare and Vercel are built in          |
| What the customer grants         | Approval of your template on their DNS provider's page | A grant to their own provider account, then approval of an exact plan |
| Record values                    | Template variables, within the template's rules        | Any records you declare, per customer or tenant                       |
| Reach                            | Nine providers listed live                             | Two built in                                                          |
| Scope                            | Applying a template                                    | Connect, plan, approve, apply, receipt, verify, cleanup               |
| License                          | Public domain spec (CC0 1.0)                           | MIT license, no per-domain fee                                        |

## When each fits

### Domain Connect fits when

- Your setup is a fixed template with a few variables.
- Your customers' DNS providers already support it and have onboarded your template.
- You want the DNS provider to own the consent screen.

### DomainKit fits when

- Record values vary per customer, tenant, or environment.
- You want the flow, the consent, and the audit inside your own app.
- Your customers are on Cloudflare or Vercel.
- You need receipts, verification, and reviewed cleanup after the first click.

## Offering both

They don't conflict. Try the automated path that fits the customer's DNS provider and keep manual
records as the last option. The order is your decision, made in your own UI. DomainKit doesn't ship
a Domain Connect adapter, and it doesn't publish templates.
[What DomainKit does and doesn't do about Domain Connect](/docs/core/domain-connect).

## FAQ

<Accordion>
  <AccordionItem title="Is Domain Connect still active?">
    Yes. As of 29 September 2026 its Templates repository had a push that day, and new templates
    were being merged.
  </AccordionItem>
  <AccordionItem title="Does DomainKit implement Domain Connect?">
    No. It has no Domain Connect adapter and doesn't publish templates.
  </AccordionItem>
  <AccordionItem title="Is DomainKit a replacement for Domain Connect?">
    No. They use different mechanisms. One relies on DNS providers onboarding your template. The
    other relies on your customer granting access to their own provider account.
  </AccordionItem>
  <AccordionItem title="Which is faster to ship?">
    It depends on your customers' DNS providers. If they already support Domain Connect and have
    onboarded your template, the protocol needs no library. If they're on Cloudflare or Vercel,
    DomainKit doesn't wait for an onboarding.
  </AccordionItem>
</Accordion>
